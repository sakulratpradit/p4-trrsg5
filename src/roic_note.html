ROIC - why it replaced ROE in our Quality score

Filed 28 Nov 2026 at Salee's request. Approved by Salee the same day ("Yes please for ROIC instead of ROI in our quality scoring as soon as possible"). Live on the dashboard: Overview matrix row 21 (ROI / ROIC %) and Quality check 27c. Figures below are board data at the 27 Nov 2026 closes.

1. The two measures

ROE  (return on equity)           = profit / shareholders' money
ROIC (return on invested capital) = operating profit after tax / (shareholders' money + borrowed money)

Both ask "how much profit does the company make on the money in the business?". The difference is which money. ROE counts only the owners' money. ROIC counts all of it, including debt.

Thai shop example. Two noodle shops each make 20,000 baht a year on a 100,000-baht shop. Shop A paid with its own money: ROE 20%, ROIC 20%. Shop B put in 20,000 baht and borrowed 80,000: its ROE jumps to about 70% (after interest), yet it is the same shop with the same profit - and more risk. ROIC still says 20%, because it counts the borrowed 80,000 as capital too.

2. Why ROE misleads

Borrowing money or buying back shares shrinks the equity that ROE divides by, so ROE rises with no better business. On our board:

3. The rule (check 27c, since 28 Nov 2026)

ROIC 20% or more    = 2 points
ROIC 10% to 20%     = 1 point
ROIC under 10%      = 0
No ROIC figure      = use ROE instead (marked * in row 27c; today SPCX, LHX, CIEN)

Why 10%: it is roughly the cost of capital - what investors demand for owning a stock. 10-year Treasury about 4.9% + an equity risk premium of 4-5% = about 9-10%. A company earning under 10% on its capital creates no value for its owners, however fast it grows. 20% or more means it earns about twice what its money costs. This links the Quality score to the same bond yield that drives our rate gates and price lines.

The score stays 0-10, and Priority = upside to fair value x Quality / 10 is unchanged.

6. How to read it - three cautions

Low ROIC after a big purchase is often temporary. AMD (Xilinx), MRVL and PANW carry large acquired goodwill in their capital, which drags ROIC down for years. Check whether the trend is rising before judging.
Heavy builders show low ROIC while the building is under way. META, AMZN and CEG are spending on data centres and power plants that do not earn yet - the same story as their thin FCF margins (10 Dec agenda, item B).
ROIC formulas differ between data sources (PLTR shows 307% on ours; others show less). Only the 10% and 20% bars matter for scoring, so the exact figure above 20% does not change any score. The earnings refresh flags any ROIC that jumps between quarters without a business reason.

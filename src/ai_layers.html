AI layers - who sells what

One page for seven board names: MRVL, AVGO, ANET, ALAB, LITE, APP, COHR. Think of an AI data centre as a city: the GPUs are the factories; everything else is the roads, power lines and telephone wires that connect them. Six of the seven sell the wiring. One (APP) sells something built on top. Added 5 Nov 2026; companion to Slide #109 "Layers of AI".

4 · Applications
APP AppLovin - sells ad placements inside mobile games and e-commerce; its AI engine (Axon) picks the ad. Gets paid by advertisers' marketing budgets, not by data-centre capex.
3 · Network (rack to rack)
ANET Arista - finished Ethernet switches with its own software (EOS). AVGO Broadcom - the Tomahawk switch chips inside those switches.
2 · Links (chip to chip, rack to rack)
ALAB Astera Labs - retimers and switches that clean up short copper links inside a rack (PCIe, CXL, Scorpio). LITE Lumentum and COHR Coherent - lasers and optical transceivers for links longer than a few metres. MRVL Marvell - the DSP chip inside every transceiver that turns electrical signals into light and back.
1 · Compute (custom AI chips)
AVGO Broadcom - designs custom AI chips (XPUs) for Google (TPU), Meta and OpenAI. MRVL Marvell - the same for Amazon (Trainium) and Microsoft (Maia). TSMC makes them.
Foundation
GPUs: NVDA, AMD · Memory: MU · Foundry: TSM · Power and cooling: VRT, ETN, GEV, VST

The one thing to remember

AVGO, MRVL, ALAB, LITE and ANET all get paid the same way - a hyperscaler decides to build a cluster. They rise and fall together (power and semis fell 9-14% together on 4 Nov). APP gets paid when an advertiser's budget grows. That is the real diversification in this list, and why APP is not "more semi exposure".

What would hurt each one: AVGO and MRVL - a hyperscaler cuts its custom-chip programme or Nvidia wins it back. LITE and COHR - a pause in optics orders or China export rules. ALAB - Nvidia integrates the retimer. ANET - InfiniBand or white-box switches win. APP - Apple/Google privacy rules, an ad-spending recession, or a short-seller report on its data use.

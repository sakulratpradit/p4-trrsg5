3. How macro feeds our rules

Rates -> rate rule. The 10-year is the price of money for every company. Above 4.5% we do not fund companies that lose money or trade on far-off earnings. The trigger to relax it is a 10-year close below 4.5%, not a headline about Fed cuts.

Index drawdown -> crash reserve. The $50,000 of SGOV is only spent if the S&P closes 15% or more below its high. Then it buys names already on the board, at or below fair value, in two halves.

Sector sell-offs -> standing lines. Individual price lines (NVDA $180, PLTR $150, AMD $470, TSM $380, AVGO $310) act on a close at or below the line, never on an intraday print, and the order is a Day LIMIT at that close with one raise to +1%.

Baht -> reporting only. FX changes the baht value of the book, not our decisions. The book is not hedged; the 5-year target is in dollars.

Politics -> the 10 Dec review. Policy news changes a rule only when it becomes a bill with co-sponsors, an executive action, or a company changing its orders or guidance. Until then it goes on the agenda, not into an order.

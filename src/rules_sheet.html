All rules on one sheet - and how a price line is set

Filed 16 Nov 2026 at Salee's request. Prices at the Fri 13 Nov 2026 close; 10-year yield 5.15%. One idea to remember: rates set how picky we are, the ranking picks the name, the price line picks the price. Companion cards: "Rate gates" (why 35x and 5.25%) and "How we rank stocks".

1. Every buy passes five checks, in this order - one "no" stops it

2. The rate ladder (check 2) - 10-year Treasury closing yield

10-year closeWhat is allowed
Below 4.5%Everything the ranking and price lines allow, including loss-making companies
4.5 - 5.0%Rate rule: no loss-making companies (RKLB, SPCX ...)
5.0 - 5.25% (now)+ Valuation gate: no new money above 35x forward earnings unless the stock has a price line (ISRG $15,618 and GEV $519 on hold)
Above 5.25%Tripwire: only price lines buy; the February money waits in SGOV

Every step reads the closing yield, never an intraday print. No rule ever forces a sale. Price lines are the only buys that survive even the tripwire, because a line is already a cheap price.

Two special rules outside the five checks: (1) Crash reserve - the $50,000 in SGOV is spent only if the S&P 500 closes 15% or more below its high, in two halves. (2) Stock alarms - a written trigger can suspend one name (NVDA today: buying suspended, four triggers, decision after the 17 Nov results).

3. Price line vs ranking - two different questions

ToolQuestion it answers
Ranking (priority = upside × quality ÷ 10)Which stock gets the next dollar
Price lineAt what price we buy it

They reinforce each other: when a stock falls to its line, its upside to fair value grows, so its rank rises at the same moment. Lines are set only on names we want most - on 12 Oct AVGO ranked #3 and NVDA #4 among names with unspent budget.

5. Proposed single method (for approval on 10 Dec)

Line = the lower of two numbers, rounded, reset after each quarter's results:
(a) Fair value × 0.70 - a 30% safety margin below the analysts' average;
(b) Forward earnings per share × the "bond-beating P/E", where bond-beating P/E = 100 ÷ (10-year yield + 0.5). At 5.15%: 100 ÷ 5.65 ≈ 17.7x - at that price the company earns about 5.65% a year on what we pay, more than the 10-year bond.

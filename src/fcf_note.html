1. The measure in one line

Free cash flow (FCF) = cash from operations - capital spending (capex)
FCF margin          = FCF / revenue x 100      (last 12 months)

Of every $100 of sales, how many dollars are left over as spare cash for the owners after running the business and paying for its investment. It is harder to dress up than profit: it is cash that actually arrived. Above 15% is strong; Visa, Broadcom and Nvidia run above 40%.

4. How to read it - three cautions

A thin margin is not always bad news. GOOGL (4.2%), META (10.4%) and AMZN (-5.3%) are low because they are spending record sums on AI data centres. The score measures today's cash, not the payoff from that spending - that is the 10 Dec question "who funds the AI spending" (item B).
A very high margin may not last. Memory makers (MU 44%, and SanDisk 60% on the watch list) are at a price peak; the market already prices those margins as temporary, which is why their forward P/E looks so low.
GEV at 38.9% is far above GE Vernova's history (single digits to low teens). Customer deposits on gas-turbine orders may be inflating it. The next earnings refresh checks it against the cash-flow statement before 10 Dec.

5. Who can keep a high margin for ten years?

Lasting - toll roads and monopolies: Visa, Mastercard, CME (payment networks and an exchange), ASML (only maker of EUV machines), Broadcom. Cyclical - memory (MU, SanDisk, SK hynix) and trading-volume businesses (Robinhood). In between - AppLovin (ad tech, few big revenue sources), Nvidia (competition will compress it eventually), Palantir (margin is fine; the 86x forward P/E prices in perfection).
